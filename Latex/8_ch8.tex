\subsection{Target Scene Reconstruction}
\label{subsec:ch8.section1.subsec1}

The Avia consistently struggled to produce accurate representations of the calibration targets. In all tests, the point clouds produced were sparse and contained significant amounts of self-shadowing, making it difficult to resolve the geometry of the targets. For all targets, it was not possible to infer the detailed geometry of the targets from the point cloud recreations. In every case, a rough estimation of two target dimensions was possible (see Tables \ref{tab:target_a} to \ref{tab:target_d}) and the overall shape of the targets could be inferred, but no detailed features of the targets could be resolved.

The large angle of incidence between the Avia and the targets led to low point densities on the target surfaces, as well as exacerbating the sensor's large beam divergence and increasing target self-shadowing. These factors combined led to distorted reconstructions of the targets that made it difficult to accurately estimate their dimensions and geometry.

The wide face of target A facing the Avia allowed for a reasonable representation of the front half of the target to be captured and a rough estimation of its length and height to be made. However, the back half of the target was self-shadowed, resulting in a significant portion of the target being unobserved and thus no accurate estimation of its width could be made. 

Every target besides target B had self-shadowing that occluded the back portions of the targets, making it impossible to estimate the target dimension parallel to the Avia's line of sight. The concave geometry and orientation of target B produced significant self-shadowing of its curved surface, making it impossible to resolve the target geometry accurately, even when many frames (15) were concatenated together. 

For all targets besides target C, the height estimations were consistently lower than the actual heights of the targets. This is likely due to the targets deforming and sagging slightly when placed on the ground, resulting in lower heights than expected. The target C height estimation was greater than expected, likely due to the sensors' beam divergence misreporting points slightly above the actual target surface.

The rough geometry of target D was able to be resolved reasonably well, despite the low point density. The orientation of the target led to minimal self-shadowing, allowing for a more accurate estimation of the target geometry.

The large angle of incidence between the Avia and the targets led to low point densities on the target surfaces, as well as exacerbating the sensor's large beam divergence and increasing target self-shadowing. The beam divergence caused points to be reported over a larger area than the actual target surface, leading to inaccuracies in the point cloud representations. The low point densities further compounded this issue, making it difficult to resolve the true shapes of the targets. The Avia alone is not well suited to accurately capturing detailed geometries of objects at oblique angles and longer ranges from a limited number of point cloud frames.

\subsection{Normals Estimation}
\label{subsec:ch8.section1.subsec2}

In all cases, the point normals estimation produced poor results. The estimated normals (Figures \ref{fig:A10Norm} to \ref{fig:D10Norm}) closely matched the geometry of the reconstructed targets, see Figures \ref{fig:A10Mesh}, to \ref{fig:D10Mesh}, but this did not add any additional useful information about the targets. The low point density and distorted geometry of the target point clouds meant that the estimated normals provided no more insight than what could already be visually observed from the target reconstructions.
