\section{Data Collection Apparatus}
\label{sec:ch4.section1}
%----------------------------------------------------

All data used in this investigation was collected using the Livox Avia. Aalto data collected using a Windows laptop and Livox viewer software, proprietary .lvx file. All other data collected using Ubuntu laptop and ROS, .bag file.

The sensor was set up to capture data using ROS (ROS Neotic) and the Livox ROS driver through a machine running Ubuntu 20.04. The sensor scans and creates point clouds at a rate of 10 Hz and the IMU collects data at a rate of 200 Hz. The captured data was saved and stored in the rosbag file format (.bag). These files contain all the recorded data, (point cloud e here).information (location and return intensity), IMU readings, and their associated timestamps.

LIDAR BEAM DIVERGENCE

The following setup was devised to test the Avia's ability to resolve basic geometric shapes and their sizes, investigate its performance when observing targets at angles similar to those of the SCALE cruise and evaluate the impact of the sensor's beam divergence.

\subsection{Kalman Filter Experiment}

A method is required to compare the output of the Kalman filter to known motion. This was achieved through the use of the Avia, a Stewart platform, and a motion capture system. To simulate the Avia recording data from a moving platform, a Stewart platform was used, and a motion capture system recorded ground-truth motion data for comparison and validation. A Stewart platform is a device that has the ability to manipulate its target platform in 6 degrees (linear translations on the X, Y and Z axes and rotation along the X, Y, and Z axes). The Stewart platform used was designed and built by Liam Clark, a former student for his Final Year Project. This platform has been in storage since 2018 having been used occasionally over the years, and has had many repairs and parts replaced. This has resulted in the platform performing irregularly and not up to the original design specifications. The use of the motion capture system overcomes this.
The motion capture system is manufactured by OptiTrack, who provided all sensors, software and supporting equipment. The system consists of 4 OptiTrack Prime 13 cameras and 3 OptiTrack Prime 13W (wide angle) cameras, and are all managed by a PC running OptiTrack's Motive software. The cameras both record data at a rate of 240 Hz, while the Prime 13 has a listed 3D accuracy of +/- 0.02 mm, and the Prime 13W has a listed 3D accuracy of +/- 0.03 mm.

The cameras are sensitive only to infrared light and tracking markers that reflect infrared light. The cameras are fixed in known locations around the target area and capture video of the target, while markers placed on the target allow the cameras to track the position of the target over time. Combining the different viewpoints of the cameras, the full motion of the target can be reconstructed.
As this experiment is only testing the attitude estimation capabilities of the Kalman filter, no translation of the platform will take place, only rotation. The Avia is attached to the Stewart Platform and rotated while recording IMU data for analysis. Motion capture markers are attached to the sensor to allow for the recording of ground-truth data.

The Avia was first rotated along single axes, first along the X-axis, and then along the Y-axis before being rotated on both the X and Y-axes, before finally being rotated along all axes (X, Y, and Z). This routine was performed twice, first with a magnitude of 10°, then with a magnitude of 5°. The frequency was set to follow a chirp function, starting at a frequency of 0.005 Hz and ending at a frequency of 0.05 Hz. Each set of rotations occurred over 60 seconds. These amplitude and frequency parameters were obtained by inspecting IMU data from the SCALE cruise and selecting test values similar to those recorded during data collection.

The co-ordinate systems of the Stewart Platform and motion capture system were not aligned, the Stewart Platform was offset from the motion capture system by -44° around its Z-axis.

\subsection{Aalto University Ice and Wave Tank}

Aalto University operates a wave and ice tank 40 m x 40 m and 2.8 m deep. The tank is equipped with wedge-type wave generators capable of producing regular and irregular waves *pamphlet cite here*. In addition to wave generators, the tank is equipped with a cooling system and sprayers for producing model-scale ice.

%----------------------------------------------------
\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{figs/Aalto_Livox.jpeg}
    \caption{\textbf{Livox Avia at Aalto University Ice and Wave Tank.}}
    \label{fig:aalto}
\end{figure}
%----------------------------------------------------

In November 2023 another Masters student (Anand Nair cite?) ran experiments in the tank to simulate the motion of a model of an individual pancake ice floe. In addition to the sensors selected by Anand for his experiments, data from the tests was collected using the Livox Avia.
The Avia was mounted on a mobile gantry above the tank, angled to face down towards the ice and water. Waves were generated and briefly propagated through open water before reaching the ice. A capacitive probe in the tank recorded the wave height over time, and was placed 5 meters in front of the wave generators. The wave data was sampled at a rate of 300 Hz.

Six tests were conducted and the wave hight was recorded over the duration of the tests. For the tests sinusoidal waves were generated with two periods, (1.6 s and 2s), and three wave heights per period, (50 mm, 75 mm and 100 mm). During the tests there was no time synchronisation between the wave logger and Livox Avia.

During the experiments, strict constraints were placed on the waves generated and their behaviours. The generated waves were all sine waves in deep water (the water depth is greater than half the largest generated wavelength, 2.8 m $>$ 2 m). The wave parameters were held constant during individual tests, and the waves were propagated straight down the tank, perpendicular to the Avia's line of sight.

\subsection{SCALE Winter 2022 Cruise}

The SCALE Winter 2022 Cruise was a research cruise aboard then Research Vessel S.A. Agulhas II that took place from the 11th to the 31st of July 2022. Amongst other data collection and research activities taking place on the vessel, 31 LiDAR scans of the open ocean and ice were taken, between the 15th and the 27th of July. Of these 31 scans, 12 contained ice and the other 19 were either setup or ocean scans. All scans of ice were taken between 58° and 59°35' South.
