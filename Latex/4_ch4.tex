\chapter{Data Collection}
\label{ch:ch4}
%====================================================

Data for this investigation were collected from two sources, the Southern oCean seAsonaL Experiment (SCALE) Winter 2022 Cruise and the Aalto University Ice and Wave Tank.

%====================================================
\section{SCALE Winter 2022 Cruise}
\label{sec:ch4.section1}
%----------------------------------------------------

The SCALE Winter 2022 Cruise was a research cruise aboard then Research Vessel S.A. Agulhas II that took place from the 11th to the 31st of July 2022. Amongst other data collection and research activities taking place on the vessel, 31 LiDAR scans of the open ocean and ice were taken, between the 15th and the 27th of July. Of these 31 scans, 12 contained ice and the other 19 were either setup or ocean scans. All scans of ice were taken between 58° and 59°35' South.

Data were collected from the port side 7th floor deck, at a height of approximately 16-17 m above the waterline. The AVIA was connected to a laptop running Ubuntu 20.04 and ROS Neotic. 
%====================================================
\section{Aalto University Ice and Wave Tank}
\label{sec:ch4.section2}
%----------------------------------------------------
Aalto University operates a wave and ice tank 40 m x 40 m and 2.8 m deep. The tank is equipped with wedge-type wave generators capable of producing regular and irregular waves *pamphlet cite here*. In addition to wave generators, the tank is equipped with a cooling system and sprayers for producing model-scale ice.

In November 2023 another Masters student (Anand Nair cite?) ran experiments in the tank to simulate the motion of a model of an individual pancake ice floe. In addition to the sensors selected by Anand for his experiments, data from the tests was collected using the Livox Avia.
The Avia was mounted on a mobile gantry above the tank, angled to face down towards the ice and water. Waves were generated and briefly propagated through open water before reaching the ice. A capacitive probe in the tank recorded the wave height over time.
